# Commission proposal — digest

- **Proposal:** `COM(2026) 504 final` (3 June 2026) — "on a framework of measures for strengthening
  the Union's semiconductor ecosystem, repealing Regulation (EU) 2023/1781 (Chips Act 2.0)", Text
  with EEA relevance
- **Legal bases:** Articles 173(3) and 114 TFEU
- **Accompanying:** SEC(2026) 504, SWD(2026) 504 (impact assessment, Parts 1–3), SWD(2026) 505
  (executive summary), Annexes 1–7 *(note: the SWD cover pages misprint the repealed act as
  "Regulation (EU) 2023/1782"; the proposal and Art 58 say 2023/1781)*
- **Base-text extracts:** [`extracts/commission/`](../extracts/commission/) (12 slices; anchors `article-N`)
- **Register entry:** [`sources/README.md`](../sources/README.md) · [library page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/proposal-chips-act-20)
- **Owner:** lead service DG CNECT; political leads President von der Leyen and EVP Virkkunen
  (Tech Sovereignty, Security and Democracy)

## Why (the Commission's case)

Per the explanatory memorandum: the EU produces **less than 10% of global semiconductors** and is
almost entirely dependent on the United States and Asia for the most advanced and leading-edge chips
below 5 nm, including AI chips; semiconductors are the world's third most traded commodity (global
revenues USD 700.9bn in 2025, expected to pass USD 1 trillion in 2026); supply shortages since 2020
(COVID-19, the Nexperia case) and "attempts to economically coerce" the EU exposed the ecosystem's
fragility — the Draghi diagnosis of declining industrial capacity. The two problems the revision
targets: **(a) overdependence on third countries for semiconductor design and manufacturing;
(b) insufficient crisis-preparedness capabilities.**

The memorandum's evaluation of the 2023 Act: significant outputs (five pilot lines, competence
centres, an embryonic design platform, over EUR 52bn committed in public and private investment
under Pillar II, the ESB coordination mechanism), but a persistent **lab-to-fab gap**, insufficient
private/late-stage capital (the EIC-Accelerator part of the Chips Fund was used up in its first two
years), demand-side weaknesses, and "too limited" insight into supply chains for crisis preparedness.
Where the 2023 Act was predominantly supply-driven, **Chips Act 2.0 places greater emphasis on
demand-side measures**, explicitly linked to CADA's data-centre/AI-Gigafactory demand for AI chips.
The impact assessment received a **negative RSB opinion** (28 January 2026), then a positive opinion
with reservations (30 March 2026); the preferred option is the "vertical (proactive) industrial
policy approach" (PO2). It also implements the **Semicon Coalition Declaration** signed by all 27
Member States ([Commission news](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/semicon-coalition-calls-reinforced-chips-act)).
The Commission's scientific evidence base is the same-day **JRC science-for-policy brief *From
dependency to resilience*** (JRC147104, 3 June 2026; linked from the Commission policy page) — framing
the package as a shift from digital regulation to assertive industrial strategy over critical
technologies ([register](../sources/README.md#commission)).

## Headline ambitions

- **Open foundry (Art 19):** under the Competitiveness Coordination Tool (CCT), a strategic project
  on advanced manufacturing "will be treated with the highest priority" — the first semiconductor
  facility in the Union **combining leading-edge node manufacturing with chiplet integration and
  advanced 3D packaging**; "pilot production could be envisaged in the period 2030–2033"
  (explanatory memorandum). The recitals add it "should operate, where relevant, on the basis of
  open access for different users".
- **Permitting:** EU permitting and design phases are on average 7.5 months longer than in competing
  jurisdictions; each year of delay adds ~5% to total project cost (illustration: ~EUR 625m on a
  EUR 20bn fab). Hence the 12-month cap (Art 21(2)).
- **Funding:** under the current MFF, Horizon Europe + Digital Europe Programme carry Initiative 2.0;
  the proposal is "designed to be compatible" with the next MFF (European Competitiveness Fund,
  FP10) without prejudging it — operational/administrative budgets for 2028–2034 "can only be
  requested at a later stage". The only quantified new expenditure is ~EUR 70m for the B2B Platform.
  No headline investment envelope appears in the proposal itself — see
  [`fault-lines.md`](fault-lines.md) on the figures circulating in secondary reporting.

## Drafting quirks in the source

Observed while reading the proposal text (verify against the committed PDF; flagged for the extracts):

- **Art 1 paragraph numbering** runs 1., 1., 2., 3. — the first two paragraphs are both numbered "1".
- **Art 30(1)** cites "Directives **2024/23/EU**, 2014/24/EU or 2014/25/EU" — apparent misprint for
  2014/23/EU.
- Cross-references to the priority-rated-order obligation **oscillate between Art 42(1) and
  Art 43(1)**: Arts 14(6)(b), 18(9) and 51(4)–(5) point to Article 43 (common purchasing), while
  Art 15(8) points to Article 42(1) (priority-rated orders) — the latter looks correct.
- **Art 42(8)** invokes "the examination procedure referred to in Article 55(6)" — committee
  procedure lives in Article 56; Article 55 is the delegation article.
- **Art 49(2)** list points are lettered (f)–(n) instead of (a)–(i); **Art 49(4)** has the
  Commission appoint Alliance members "in accordance with the criteria set out in paragraph 4"
  (a self-reference; the criteria are in paragraph 3).
- **Art 58** runs its two paragraphs together ("…is repealed. 2. References to…").
- The memorandum states the **Commission** "is empowered to activate the crisis stage by means of an
  implementing act", but operative **Art 39(3) gives activation to the Council acting by qualified
  majority** on a Commission proposal (the memorandum elsewhere describes the Council route
  correctly).
- The memorandum dates the Semicon Coalition Declaration **29 September 2025** — confirmed by the
  [Commission news item](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/semicon-coalition-calls-reinforced-chips-act)
  (the **5 September 2025** date is the opening of the public consultation / call for evidence, a
  separate event; some secondary reporting conflates the two).

These are the kind of points a first Presidency compromise text typically cleans up — useful markers
for diffing the first ST version.
