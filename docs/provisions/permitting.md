# Fast-track permitting and the one stop shop (Chapter III s2, Arts 21–25)

> **Baseline: Commission proposal COM(2026) 504.** Operative text:
> [extracts — Ch III s2, Arts 21–25](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_permitting-art21-25.md).
> No co-legislator position yet.

## What the proposal does

- **Art 21** — the permit-granting procedure for European semiconductor technology initiatives and
  strategic projects **shall not exceed 12 months** from a complete application to the one stop
  shop; where national law knows such a status, projects get the **"highest national significance"**
  treatment —
  [Art 21](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_permitting-art21-25.md#article-21).
- **Art 22** — Member States designate a **one stop shop** running a **single permit-granting
  procedure based on a single application** covering all required permits; completeness must be
  acknowledged (or missing information requested) within 45 days, with one bounded second request;
  cross-border projects require one-stop-shop coordination —
  [Art 22](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_permitting-art21-25.md#article-22).
- **Art 23** — ESTIs and strategic projects count as "strategic projects contributing to resilience"
  under the pending **Regulation on speeding-up environmental assessments**; no duplicate
  studies/permits —
  [Art 23](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_permitting-art21-25.md#article-23).
- **Art 24** — a national **single access portal** for the single application, built on the pending
  **European Business Wallets** Regulation (automated routing, status tracking, deadline
  compliance, data re-use) —
  [Art 24](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_permitting-art21-25.md#article-24).
- **Art 25** — centralised online information (permitting, financing, business support), mirrored on
  a Commission website —
  [Art 25](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_permitting-art21-25.md#article-25).

## The Commission's case

EU permitting and design phases run on average **7.5 months longer** than in competing
jurisdictions; each year of delay adds ~5% to total project cost — illustratively ~EUR 625m on a
EUR 20bn advanced fab (explanatory memorandum). The 2023 Act asked only for "efficient" national
treatment; the hard 12-month cap, one stop shop and portal are new.

## Why it's contested

Hard deadlines and mandatory one stop shops cut into national/regional planning and
environmental-procedure autonomy, and the design depends on **two pending proposals**
(environmental-assessment speed-up; European Business Wallets) whose own timelines are open —
expect softening attempts in Council, as in other recent permitting files — see
[`fault-lines.md`](../fault-lines.md).
