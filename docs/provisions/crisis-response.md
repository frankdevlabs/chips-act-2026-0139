# Alerts, crisis stage and the emergency toolbox (Chapter IV s2–s3, Arts 37–43)

> **Baseline: Commission proposal COM(2026) 504.** Operative text:
> [extracts — Ch IV s2+s3, Arts 37–43](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_alerts-crisis-response-art37-43.md).
> Monitoring (Arts 33–36, incl. the B2B Supply Chain Platform) is in
> [extracts — Ch IV s1](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_monitoring-art33-36.md).
> No co-legislator position yet.

## What the proposal does

- **Art 37 — alerts and preventive action**: national competent authorities must alert the
  Commission of serious supply-disruption risks; the Commission convenes an extraordinary ESB
  meeting, may consult third countries, and Member States may run preventive **joint procurement**
  (under Directives 2014/24/EU and 2014/25/EU) —
  [Art 37](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_alerts-crisis-response-art37-43.md#article-37).
- **Art 38 — preventive information gathering**: Commission requests for information to the **B2B
  Supply Chain Platform** or to individual undertakings *not* participating in it (Platform
  participants are exempt); necessity/proportionality limits, penalties for false answers —
  [Art 38](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_alerts-crisis-response-art37-43.md#article-38).
- **Art 39 — activation of the crisis stage**: two cumulative conditions (serious supply-chain
  disruption causing significant shortages + serious detrimental effect on critical sectors,
  Annex V). On a Commission proposal after consulting the ESB, **the Council activates the crisis
  stage by implementing act, acting by qualified majority**; maximum 12 months, prolongable;
  reporting to the ESB and the European Parliament at least every three months —
  [Art 39](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_alerts-crisis-response-art37-43.md#article-39).
  *(The memorandum at one point says the Commission activates the crisis stage by implementing act —
  the operative text says the Council; see the
  [drafting quirks](../commission-proposal.md#drafting-quirks-in-the-source).)*
- **Art 40 — emergency toolbox**: gateway to Arts 41–43, restricted to affected critical sectors,
  proportionality and SME-burden limits, EP/Council information duties —
  [Art 40](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_alerts-crisis-response-art37-43.md#article-40).
- **Art 41 — mandatory information gathering**: the Commission may require **any undertaking along
  the supply chain** to disclose production capabilities, capacities and disruptions; fines for
  incorrect/late answers ([Art 51](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_confidentiality-penalties-art50-54.md#article-51):
  up to EUR 300 000); third-country information requests must be mirrored to the Commission —
  [Art 41](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_alerts-crisis-response-art37-43.md#article-41).
- **Art 42 — priority-rated orders**: the Commission may require **ESTIs and strategic projects**
  (and other undertakings that accepted the possibility with public support) to accept and
  prioritise orders of crisis-relevant products, **taking precedence over private/public performance
  obligations**; pass-through for undertakings under third-country priority orders; last-resort
  decision at "fair and reasonable price", hardship/incapacity opt-outs, contractual-liability
  shield; periodic penalty payments up to 1.5% of daily turnover for non-compliance —
  [Art 42](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_alerts-crisis-response-art37-43.md#article-42).
- **Art 43 — common purchasing**: on request of two or more Member States, the **Commission acts as
  central purchasing body** for crisis-relevant products for critical sectors, contracting under the
  Financial Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2024/2509; deployment/resale stays with the participating
  Member States —
  [Art 43](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_alerts-crisis-response-art37-43.md#article-43).

## What changed vs the 2023 Act

The activation conditions are stated as unaltered ("as a result of co-legislators agreement", per
the memorandum), but the surrounding machinery is strengthened: the **B2B Platform** replaces ad hoc
crisis data calls (participants gain an RFI exemption), preventive RFIs are formalised, and the
third-country priority-order pass-through (Art 42(3)) is new. The defence-sector regime of
Regulation (EU) 2025/2643 Chapter VII runs in parallel (recital 98).

## Why it's contested

These powers — mandatory disclosures backed by fines, orders overriding contracts, centralised
purchasing — were the most contested part of the 2023 Chips Act and limit Charter Arts 16–17
freedoms (the memorandum devotes its fundamental-rights section to justifying exactly this). Expect
industry pressure for narrower duties, stronger confidentiality and compensation, and Member-State
attention to the Council-trigger and national-security carve-outs — see
[`fault-lines.md`](../fault-lines.md).
