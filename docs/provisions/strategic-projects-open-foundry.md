# Strategic projects and the open foundry (Chapter III s1, Arts 13–20)

> **Baseline: Commission proposal COM(2026) 504.** Operative text:
> [extracts — Ch III s1, Arts 13–20](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_manufacturing-capabilities-art13-20.md).
> No co-legislator position yet.

## What the proposal does

- **Art 13** — ESTIs and strategic projects are declared **in the public interest**; Member States
  and the Commission may support them (without prejudice to Arts 107–108 TFEU), incl. via the
  proposed European Competitiveness Fund —
  [Art 13](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_manufacturing-capabilities-art13-20.md#article-13).
- **Arts 14–15** — **European semiconductor technology initiatives (ESTIs)**: carried out by
  **domestic undertakings** (EU seat/ownership-and-control, with FTA/GPA extensions, Art 2(31));
  must qualify as **first-of-a-kind**; requirements include supply-chain de-risking, an assurance
  against extraterritorial third-country public-service obligations, continued EU innovation
  investment, talent-pipeline support and (for production facilities) participation in the B2B
  Supply Chain Platform. Commission decision within three months, with ESB opinion. ESTIs replace
  the 2023 Act's "integrated production facilities" and "open EU foundries" (which survive
  transitionally, Art 59).
- **Arts 16–18** — **strategic projects**: recognised by the Commission where they add significant
  Union value, are carried out by domestic undertakings, have a clear cross-border dimension and
  strengthen technological sovereignty
  ([Art 16](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_manufacturing-capabilities-art13-20.md#article-16));
  participation limited to Union entities, with a security-conditioned third-country derogation.
  **Art 17**: the Commission decides **priority areas** on the basis of the indicative technological
  areas in **Annex II** (amendable by delegated act) —
  [Art 17](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_manufacturing-capabilities-art13-20.md#article-17).
  Designation runs through Chips JU funding calls (Art 18); a designation decision states whether
  the project also qualifies as first-of-a-kind.
- **Art 19 — the open foundry**: the Commission "shall treat a strategic project for a **Union-based
  open foundry for advanced semiconductor manufacturing** with the **highest priority**" — a
  facility or coordinated multi-site network in the Union capable of manufacturing *and packaging*
  advanced semiconductor technologies, taking offtaker requirements (Art 8) into account —
  [Art 19](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_manufacturing-capabilities-art13-20.md#article-19).
  Per the explanatory memorandum: under the **Competitiveness Coordination Tool**, the first EU
  facility combining **leading-edge node manufacturing with chiplet integration and advanced 3D
  packaging**; "pilot production could be envisaged in the period 2030–2033". The recitals add it
  should operate "where relevant, on the basis of open access for different users" and could be
  complemented by strategic projects on memory, design and other technologies.
- **Art 20** — financing coordination: Commission crowd-in activities; ESB advice on financing
  (EIB, national promotional banks, export credit agencies, Union programmes) —
  [Art 20](../../extracts/commission/COM-2026-504_manufacturing-capabilities-art13-20.md#article-20).

## First-of-a-kind, widened

The first-of-a-kind concept (the 2023 Act's state-aid hook) is **clarified to cover the entire value
chain**: manufacturing facilities, equipment and key-component/materials production, electronics
manufacturing and integration (printed circuit boards, advanced packaging substrates,
assembly/testing) and manufacturing-centred chip design (Art 2(10)–(11), (17)). First-of-a-kind
assessment will also apply to national co-funding of strategic projects, in line with competition
rules (memorandum).

## Why it's contested

Status without money: the regulation prioritises the open foundry but the financing sits in the
un-agreed next MFF/ECF and national aid; feasibility, operatorship and state-aid questions are the
package's biggest open front. Rights attached to the status (priority access, fast-track permits)
come with obligations (priority-rated orders, [Art 42](crisis-response.md); Platform participation) —
see [`fault-lines.md`](../fault-lines.md). Investment figures circulating in secondary reporting
(~€120bn / ~€30bn for the foundry) are **indicative, not in the proposal**.
