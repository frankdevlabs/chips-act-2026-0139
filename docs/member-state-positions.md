## All 27 Member States — Semicon Coalition Declaration (pre-legislative baseline)

On **29 September 2025** the **Semicon Coalition** — the ministers of all 27 Member States — signed a
Declaration calling for a reinforced, forward-looking **Chips Act 2.0** to strengthen Europe's
position in the global semiconductor value chain
([Commission news](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/semicon-coalition-calls-reinforced-chips-act) ·
[timeline](../TIMELINE.md)). The explanatory memorandum presents the proposal as implementing this
Declaration. Baseline support for the exercise — it says nothing about
where delegations will land on the contested provisions in [`fault-lines.md`](fault-lines.md).

## Netherlands

**Non-paper "An Integrated Approach for the EU Tech Sovereignty Package"** (The Hague, 17 April 2026;
tabled around the informal Telecom Council of 20 April 2026) —
[register](../sources/README.md) · [Tweede Kamer 2026D18815](https://www.tweedekamer.nl/downloads/document?id=2026D18815)

- Core idea: organise the package around a **"Sovereign Tech Stack"** with a self-reinforcing
  demand–supply loop across the stack's layers (raw materials/**hardware**/infrastructure up to
  software, data and applications), with **open source as cross-cutting enabler**; builds on the
  "EuroStack vision of European digital sovereignty".
- Covers the whole package — the **hardware/chips layer included**: semiconductors sit at the base
  of the stack, and the demand–supply-loop logic (EU demand pulling EU supply) maps directly onto
  the Chips Act 2.0's demand-side instruments
  ([chips-initiative-demand](provisions/chips-initiative-demand.md)).
- The same document is registered in the sibling CADA tracker (file 2026/0138); CADA-specific
  content is analysed there, not here.

**BNC-fiche "Chips Act 2.0"** — the formal Dutch government position on COM(2026) 504
(Kamerstuk 22 112, nr. 4407; cover letter The Hague, 10 July 2026; published 22 July 2026) —
[register](../sources/README.md) · [officielebekendmakingen kst-1263577](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-1263577.html).
Lead ministry EZK; Council formation Competitiveness; QMV; EP co-decision. A separate fiche in the
same transmission covers the sibling CADA (2026/0138) — analysed in that repo, not here.

*Grondhouding (overall stance):* **positive on the main lines** and consistent with the NL non-paper
and the Semicon Coalition Declaration. Positive on **competence** (Arts 4, 2a, 6b, 114(1), 173(3) TFEU)
and **subsidiarity**; positive on **proportionality but with caveats** (financial/co-financing design and
the Commission's crisis powers). The kabinet's headline concern is an **"inconsistent line"**: the
proposal reads as too one-sidedly defensive — focused on resilience and production capacity — while
*indispensability* (onmisbaarheid) and *prosperity* fade to the background; it wants demand-steering
**and** indispensability equally and proportionately anchored across the Regulation and its instruments.
It also notes the package covers the whole AI technology stack except chips as an end-market, and will
plead (with the CADA fiche) for complementary action so the full stack is covered.

Provision-level positions (deep-linked to the operative extracts via the analysis pages):

- **Chips for Europe Initiative 2.0 / Grand Challenges / Demand Accelerators**
  ([chips-initiative-demand](provisions/chips-initiative-demand.md)) — positive on the revised design and
  on continuing the Chips JU, Pilot Lines and Design Platform; explicitly welcomes the operational
  objectives on **integrated photonics and quantum chips**. Positive on Grand Challenges but wants
  clarity on the selected priority areas and governance, and a **more prominent Member-State role** in
  their selection/prioritisation via the ESB. Key ask: **advanced production machines & equipment**
  should be recognised as a Grand Challenge in their own right — the kabinet will push to add this to
  **Annex I** (a proven, unique EU strength, e.g. ASML). Welcomes Demand Accelerators / Demand Forum
  but questions the practical design, the role of the Industrial Alliance and the financing.
- **ESTI/FOAK status and the *domestic undertaking* definition**
  ([strategic-projects-open-foundry](provisions/strategic-projects-open-foundry.md)) — welcomes the
  simplification into a single ESTI/FOAK status; wants advanced production machines clearly placed
  within the FOAK definition. Finds the **>50% ownership criterion for a "domestic undertaking" too
  strict**, will assess the impact on NL firms, and stresses the definition must not shut out trusted,
  like-minded partners (WTO/trade-agreement compatibility).
- **Strategic Projects & the Art 19 Advanced Manufacturing Project**
  ([strategic-projects-open-foundry](provisions/strategic-projects-open-foundry.md)) — supports the
  cross-border approach and the resilience/prosperity/indispensability + economic-viability criteria,
  but raises **serious governance concerns**: the Commission gets a disproportionately large role in
  selection, and **Art 19 fixes the Advanced Manufacturing (open-foundry) project directly as a strategic
  project**, effectively bypassing the described Member-State approval process — "inconsistent" with the
  Strategic-Projects procedure. Wants a more transparent, ESB-steered selection on **excellence** and
  contribution to indispensability/resilience, with national co-financing **not decisive**; will push a
  prominent place for NL firms.
- **Fast-track permitting / *overriding public interest***
  ([permitting](provisions/permitting.md)) — welcomes ESTI/Strategic-Projects as overriding public
  interest and the streamlined permitting, but the speed must reckon with the reality of the
  *omgevingsdiensten*. Positive on a single one-stop-shop and the European Business Wallet, but the
  role split between the *single access portal*, the *Business Wallet* and the *one-stop-shop* is unclear;
  permitting coordination needs customisation and Member States must keep the room to decide which tier
  (Rijk/province/municipality) leads and to maintain existing national systems (the Omgevingswet
  coordination regime).
- **European Semiconductor Regions of Excellence**
  ([regions-of-excellence](provisions/regions-of-excellence.md)) — positive; fits the strong NL regional
  ecosystems. Wants clarity on **Art 26(3)** — scope and implications for regions that do **not** carry
  the label and for innovative firms outside the selected clusters, which must not be disadvantaged or
  excluded.
- **Strategic procurement, the non-financial resilience criterion and *risk-prone sectors***
  ([chips-initiative-demand](provisions/chips-initiative-demand.md) · [crisis-response](provisions/crisis-response.md))
  — supports strategic procurement and a non-financial resilience criterion in principle, but is
  **critical of the vagueness**: undefined *'sufficient risk'* triggers and missing thresholds leave it
  unclear when obligations bite and who holds final competence, and of the reliance on voluntariness —
  it misses a **heavier/direct obligation for large-scale European projects (AI gigafactories)**. Wants
  the interplay with CSA II, NIS2 and the CRA made clear, the depth of required supply-chain analyses
  bounded, and duplication with other sectoral law (incl. the IAA) avoided.
- **Monitoring & crisis (Pillar III) / B2B platform**
  ([crisis-response](provisions/crisis-response.md)) — welcomes continued crisis-preparedness but wants
  the new crisis mechanisms underpinned against existing ones (IMERA) and complementary to the Critical
  Raw Materials Centre (ResourceEU); wants clearer pre-crisis/crisis phase delineation and thresholds.
  Positive on the **B2B Semiconductor Supply Chain Platform** provided broad industry buy-in, with
  clarity on data-misuse safeguards, who runs it, and its relation to the Economic Security Information
  Hub. Crisis powers (mandatory information requests, priority-rated orders) are **proportionate only if
  strictly limited to extreme emergencies and closely coordinated with Member States via the ESB**.
- **ESB governance** — welcomes the more strategic positioning of the **European Semiconductor Board**
  and greater industry involvement, but insists the ESB have a **decisive steering role**; the very
  detailed Annexes already pre-empt that steering, and the kabinet will push that the Annexes not
  sideline the Board.
- **Delegated vs implementing acts** — agrees the Annex I–III amendments belong in **delegated acts**
  (Art 290), but argues **Art 32** (and Art 31(2)) — designating risk-sensitive sectors and mitigating
  measures — fills in the substantive scope of the Regulation and should therefore arguably be a
  **delegated** rather than an implementing act.

*Financial / regulatory-burden read:* no direct MFF reallocation before 2027; the impact assessment's
~€40bn public-private for advanced (front-end) manufacturing and ~€15bn combined public means (EU+MS,
at 30–40% aid intensity) are noted, with the **ECF flagged as the primary EU source** and future
operational funding tied to the MFF 2028-2034 negotiations — the kabinet will not pre-empt that. It
flags **national co-financing** pressure (Chips JU matching; a €70m B2B platform; regional investment
plans for the Region-of-Excellence label; permitting/monitoring costs for provinces, municipalities and
CBS). For business it estimates **~48–72 large NL firms** (10–15% of the Commission's 483 large critical
firms) facing one-off ~€4.8–7.2m and recurring ~€2.4–3.6m/yr from the B2B platform. It **shares the ATR
(Adviescollege toetsing regeldruk)** concerns — that the Commission commit to mapping the total burden of
the subsidy modules, and only extend procurement duties to NIS2/CAII firms once that burden is mapped.

**National context:**

- The kabinet flags that the proposal drew **negative reactions from foreign governments** warning of
  protectionism; it wants the access/definition/security-of-supply criteria kept transparent,
  proportionate and WTO/trade-agreement compliant ([fault-lines](fault-lines.md)).
- The annotated agenda for the formal Telecom Council of 9 June 2026 (Kamerstuk series 21501-33)
  predates the package presentation and contains no Chips Act 2.0 appraisal.
- The NL semiconductor ecosystem (ASML/NXP, Brainport) makes this delegation a natural protagonist
  on this file — sector signals are tracked in [`stakeholders.md`](stakeholders.md).
- The NL digital-government portal published a package announcement
  ("Meer digitale autonomie met het Tech Sovereignty Package", 5 June 2026,
  [digitaleoverheid.nl](https://www.digitaleoverheid.nl/nieuws/meer-digitale-autonomie-met-het-tech-sovereignty-package/))
  welcoming the Commission package and naming **Chips Act 2.0** / advanced semiconductors as one of
  its four elements. A government-communication signal only — it restates the Commission framing and
  the Parliament/Council next step, adding no new national position beyond the 17 April 2026 non-paper.
