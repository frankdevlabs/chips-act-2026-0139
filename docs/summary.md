# Summary — what this file does

The **Chips Act 2.0** — COM(2026) 504 final, file 2026/0139 (COD) — is the Commission's proposal
(3 June 2026) to repeal and replace the 2023 European Chips Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/1781) with a
strengthened framework for the Union's semiconductor ecosystem. It is one of the two legislative
halves of the **European Technological Sovereignty Package**, presented together with the Cloud and
AI Development Act (CADA, file 2026/0138, tracked elsewhere), the EU Open Source Strategy and a
Strategic Roadmap for Digitalisation and AI in Energy. The Commission's framing: the EU produces
less than 10% of global semiconductors and is almost entirely dependent on the United States and
Asia for the most advanced chips below 5 nanometres — including AI chips — while supply shocks
(COVID-19, the Nexperia case) showed how those dependencies can be weaponised.

In plain language, the proposal (60 articles, eight Chapters — it repeals 2023/1781, Art 58, with a
correlation table in Annex VII) does five things:

1. **Chips for Europe Initiative 2.0** (Arts 3–12) — continues the 2023 Act's pilot lines, design
   platform, quantum-chip and competence-centre work, adds photonic integrated circuits, and bolts
   on demand-side instruments: **Grand Challenges**, **Demand Accelerators**, a **Demand Forum** and
   chip innovation procurement — see
   [provisions/chips-initiative-demand.md](provisions/chips-initiative-demand.md).
2. **Strategic projects and an open foundry** (Arts 13–20) — replaces the 2023 Act's "integrated
   production facilities / open EU foundries" with **European semiconductor technology initiatives**
   (first-of-a-kind, carried out by domestic undertakings) and Commission-designated **strategic
   projects** in Annex II priority areas; a Union-based **open foundry for advanced semiconductor
   manufacturing** must be treated "with the highest priority" (Art 19) — see
   [provisions/strategic-projects-open-foundry.md](provisions/strategic-projects-open-foundry.md).
3. **Fast-track permitting** (Arts 21–25) — permit-granting for those initiatives and projects may
   not exceed **12 months**, through a national **one stop shop** and a single access portal using
   European Business Wallets — see [provisions/permitting.md](provisions/permitting.md).
4. **European Semiconductor Regions of Excellence** (Arts 26–29) — a Commission-awarded label for
   regions with a credible Semiconductor Region Investment Plan, plus a network of labelled regions —
   see [provisions/regions-of-excellence.md](provisions/regions-of-excellence.md).
5. **Monitoring and crisis response** (Arts 33–43) — strategic mapping, a Business-to-Business
   Semiconductor Supply Chain Platform ("digital twin"), alerts, and a **crisis stage** (activated by
   the Council by qualified majority) unlocking mandatory information requests, **priority-rated
   orders** and Commission-led **common purchasing** — see
   [provisions/crisis-response.md](provisions/crisis-response.md). Supply-chain resilience also gets
   a peacetime leg: security-of-supply declarations in public procurement for critical sectors
   (Arts 30–32).

Governance continues via the **European Semiconductor Board** (Arts 44–47) and a newly formalised
**Industrial Alliance for Semiconductors** (Art 49). Status already granted under the 2023 Act
survives transitionally (Art 59).
